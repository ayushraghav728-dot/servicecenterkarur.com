LED TV Repair in Dindigul

Standard LED TVs are the most common televisions found in Dindigul homes. They use an LCD glass panel illuminated by an array of LED backlight strips placed behind or along the edges of the display.

Common repair problems:

  • Sound is loud and clear, but the picture is completely black
  • Red standby light blinks continuously and the TV fails to turn on
  • Flickering screen, dim picture, or uneven dark patches
  • No power after sudden power cuts or voltage surges

What technician checks:

  • Tests LED backlight strip voltages with an LED backlight tester
  • Checks 12V and 24V DC output rails on the SMPS power supply board
  • Checks motherboard LVDS signal lines and inverter boost driver

Parts involved: LED backlight strips, SMPS power board, mainboard, inverter driver, LVDS cable.
When needed: Backlight strip replacement when screen goes dark; board component repair when power fails.

LCD TV Repair in Dindigul

Traditional LCD televisions use cold cathode fluorescent lamps (CCFL tubes) rather than LED diodes to illuminate the screen. These older units have heavier bodies and high-voltage inverter transformer boards.

Common repair problems:

  • Display lights up for 2 seconds and goes totally black (inverter shutdown)
  • Screen has a reddish or pinkish tint when switched on
  • Humming or buzzing sound coming from the rear cabinet
  • TV takes 10 to 15 minutes to show picture after turning on

What technician checks:

  • High-voltage inverter board transformer coils and capacitors
  • CCFL lamp tube continuity and end-electrode condition
  • Power board secondary filter capacitors for bulging or leaks

Parts involved: CCFL tubes, inverter board, high-voltage transformers, power supply board, T-Con board.
When needed: Inverter board repair or tube replacement when display cuts off after starting.

Smart TV Repair in Dindigul

Smart TVs have built-in operating systems, Wi-Fi antennas, Ethernet ports, and streaming apps. They connect directly to home internet for YouTube, OTT platforms, and mobile screen casting.

Common repair problems:

  • TV gets stuck endlessly on the brand startup logo
  • Continuous restart loop (boot loop) every few seconds
  • Wi-Fi network not connecting, dropping frequently, or disabled
  • Apps freezing, crashing back to home screen, or not loading

What technician checks:

  • Mainboard flash memory IC (eMMC) stability and firmware state
  • DC voltage supply lines to internal Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module
  • System recovery mode, cache partitions, and motherboard voltages

Parts involved: Smart motherboard, eMMC flash memory, Wi-Fi module, Bluetooth receiver, IR sensor.
When needed: Firmware re-flashing when OS freezes; hardware module replacement if Wi-Fi or ports fail.

Common TV Problems We Check

Doorstep electronic troubleshooting for power boards, backlight arrays, logic boards, audio modules, and connectivity in Dindigul.

Power Problem

TV Not Turning On / No Power

What customer notices: The TV does not respond to remote or cabinet power button. Standby indicator light is completely off or dead.
Possible reasons: Blown internal glass fuse, voltage surge damage, faulty power supply board (SMPS), burnt rectifier diodes, or failed main power cord.
What technician checks: Tests main power cable, input fuse continuity, SMPS standby voltages (5V, 12V), and surge protection varistors.
Standby Fault

Standby Light Blinking (Won't Start)

What customer notices: Red standby light is lit or blinks repeatedly when pressing remote power, but the television never turns on.
Possible reasons: Protection circuit tripped due to shorted secondary line, weak power board capacitors, or motherboard power-on signal failure.
What technician checks: Counts the blink code pattern, tests motherboard 3.3V standby line, and checks power-on signal voltage from mainboard to SMPS.
Display Problem

Sound Coming but Screen is Blank (No Picture)

What customer notices: News, movie dialogue, or music plays normally through internal speakers, but the screen stays totally dark.
Possible reasons: Burnt-out LED backlight strips inside the display panel, faulty backlight driver on power board, or blown backlight fuse.
What technician checks: Shines a mobile torch close to the screen to look for faint background imagery, and tests LED driver voltage with a tester.
Backlight Issue

Picture is Dark / Screen Flickering

What customer notices: The screen image looks very dull, has dark shadowy patches on one side, or flickers continuously while watching.
Possible reasons: Weakening LED diodes in backlight strips, failing backlight boost capacitor, or unstable power supply voltage rails.
What technician checks: Measures current draw on backlight lines, inspects LED driver circuit, and checks for degraded LED strip connections.
Screen Lines

Lines Appearing on Screen / Half Screen Display

What customer notices: Thin colored vertical or horizontal lines run across the screen, or one vertical half of the screen is dark/frozen.
Possible reasons: T-Con logic board failure, loose or oxidized LVDS ribbon cable, or damaged panel COF (chip-on-film) bond.
What technician checks: Cleans and reseats LVDS flex ribbon connectors, tests T-Con DC-DC converter voltages (VGH, VGL, VDD), and checks panel bonds.
Audio Fault

No Sound / Distorted Sound

What customer notices: Picture is completely clear, but there is zero sound from the TV, or audio sounds harsh, muffled, or cracked.
Possible reasons: Torn internal speaker paper cone, burnt speaker voice coil, defective audio amplifier IC on motherboard, or headphone jack short.
What technician checks: Measures speaker coil resistance with an ohmmeter, inspects speaker wiring, and checks audio amplifier chip output signals.
Port Fault

HDMI / USB Port Not Working

What customer notices: DTH set-top box, gaming console, or pendrive connected via HDMI/USB shows "No Signal" or device is not detected.
Possible reasons: Broken or bent physical connector pins, dry solder joints, or HDMI switch controller IC damaged by electrical static.
What technician checks: Inspects physical connector pins, tests 5V USB/HDMI power line, and tests signal lines from port to mainboard processor.
Smart TV OS

TV Stuck on Logo / Restarting Repeatedly

What customer notices: Smart Android TV switches on, shows brand logo, and freezes there indefinitely or reboots over and over.
Possible reasons: Corrupted flash memory (eMMC), crashed firmware update, failing processor power line, or shorted peripheral module.
What technician checks: Enters system recovery menu, tests motherboard voltages, disconnects Wi-Fi module to isolate shorts, and checks firmware.
Connectivity

Wi-Fi, Bluetooth & Apps Not Opening

What customer notices: Smart TV cannot detect Wi-Fi network, internet keeps disconnecting, Bluetooth remote stops working, or apps crash.
Possible reasons: Defective internal Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module card, loose internal antenna wire, outdated app cache, or software glitch.
What technician checks: Tests 3.3V supply to the internal Wi-Fi card, inspects antenna connectors, checks network settings, and clears cache memory.
Sensor Fault

Remote Not Responding / Sensor Issue

What customer notices: TV does not react to remote buttons even with new batteries, but manual cabinet buttons work properly.
Possible reasons: Defective IR receiver sensor eye on TV, loose sensor ribbon cable, or remote control transmitter crystal failure.
What technician checks: Verifies remote IR signal with camera tester, measures 3.3V/5V DC supply to TV's IR sensor board, and tests sensor output.
Board Fault

Overheating / TV Switching Off Automatically

What customer notices: TV runs normally for 15 to 30 minutes, gets very hot on the back panel, and suddenly switches off into standby mode.
Possible reasons: Processor heatsink thermal paste dried out, failing power supply regulator under heat load, or swollen electrolytic capacitors.
What technician checks: Thermal check on processor and power MOSFETs, inspects power board capacitors under operating temperature, and services heatsink.
Display Panel

Display-Related Problems & Distortions

What customer notices: Screen shows inverted solarized colors, ghost images, white display screen with no graphics, or slow motion visual lag.
Possible reasons: T-Con gamma voltage chip failure, improper panel voltage reference, or loose LVDS cable contact pins.
What technician checks: Tests gamma voltage ladder on T-Con board, checks LVDS cable seated condition, and inspects timing controller voltages.

TV Repair Parts We Check and Replace

Technicians inspect faulty components at your home and provide transparent guidance before repair or replacement:

Power Supply Board (SMPS)

Converts 230V AC household electricity into regulated low-voltage DC lines (12V, 5V, 3.3V, 24V) for the motherboard and backlight driver. Solves dead TV, blown fuse, and power fluctuation damage.

Mainboard / Motherboard

The central brain of the television, processing video input, audio decoding, Android Smart OS, HDMI signals, and user controls. Solves logo freeze, restart loops, no sound, and dead video output.

LED Backlight Strips

Rows of bright LED diodes positioned behind or along the edges of the display glass. Solves sound coming with black screen, dim display, uneven dark shadows, and screen flickering.

T-Con Board (Timing Controller)

Translates video signals from the motherboard into row-and-column pixel data for the glass panel. Solves white screen, vertical lines, half-screen display, solarized colors, and ghost images.

Electronic Component Servicing

Component-level replacement of burnt power MOSFETs, filter capacitors, diodes, resistors, voltage regulators, and integrated circuits (ICs) on power boards and motherboards.

Ribbon Cables & LVDS Connectors

High-density flexible flat cables connecting the motherboard to the T-Con board and display panel. Solves display lines, signal flickers, and loose contact caused by moisture or vibration.

Internal Speakers & Voice Coils

Stereo loudspeaker units mounted inside the bottom television cabinet. Solves buzzing sound, jarred audio, low volume, or total loss of audio while picture remains clear.

Ports & Connectivity Modules

Physical HDMI ports, USB ports, AV terminals, internal Wi-Fi module cards, Bluetooth receivers, and remote IR sensor boards. Solves set-top box "No Signal" and wireless network dropouts.

Model-Dependent TV Parts Note

Depending on the TV model, brand, and screen size, the technician may check the parts listed above. Not every television contains every part (for instance, smaller LED TVs often use combined 3-in-1 combo boards, while large 4K Smart TVs use separate power, main, and T-Con boards).

In most LED and LCD TVs, if the standby light turns on and audio plays normally but the screen stays completely pitch black, the internal LED backlight strips have burnt out. It can also happen due to a faulty T-Con logic board or a loose LVDS ribbon cable. Our local technician tests the backlight with a diagnostic tester to confirm the exact component before quoting.

When channel dialogue or music plays clearly through the speakers but there is zero picture on screen, 90% of the time the LED backlight strip array behind the display panel has failed. You can test this at home: hold a mobile flashlight very close to the screen while a video is playing. If you can see faint moving shadows in the background, the screen panel is working and only the backlight strips need replacement.
